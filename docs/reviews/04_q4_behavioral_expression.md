# Review: 04_q4_behavioral_expression.tex (Q4)

## Assessment

The section answers its question rather than cataloguing studies: it separates response types by cost, distinguishes graded intensity from categorical action selection (marmots versus prairie dogs), isolates context and experience with a single stimulus (Mongeau), and closes on the outline's synthesis question about laboratory proxies. Guardrails are largely respected (Mongeau, prairie dogs, 22-kHz freezing, Kittendorf as adjacent). The main weaknesses are heavy overlap with Q2, Q3, and the synthesis, one citation whose endpoint may not support the claim, and use of a visual-cue study the outline confines to the introduction.

- Final paragraph: recast the two gaps (acoustic-only flight initiation distance; state dependence) as review-search conclusions ("We found no study…"), as the map's documented-gaps note requires.
- Added `% VERIFY` markers for DillHoutman1989 (paragraph 3) and KittendorfDantzer2021 (paragraph 4), both not full-text checked.

## Needs author decision

1. **Location:** Paragraph 5 and paragraph 3 versus Q2 (lines on EilamEtAl1999, OlszynskiEtAl2020, KimEtAl2010, ParsanaEtAl2012a), Q3 (ParsanaEtAl2012a, EilamEtAl1999), and the synthesis (`08_synthesis.tex`, paragraph beginning "Interpretation, in turn, does not fix the action").
   **Issue:** The synthesis paragraph repeats Q4 paragraphs 3–4 nearly verbatim (Eilam, Mongeau, the YdenbergDill1986 sentence, BaackSwitzer2000, JayneEtAl2015); the rat and spiny-mouse dissociations appear in Q2, Q3, and Q4.
   **Recommendation:** Keep the evidence in Q4 and have the synthesis refer back by `\secref{sec:q4}`; shorten Q4 paragraph 5 to the outcome-set argument with a cross-reference to Q2/Q3.
2. **Location:** Paragraph 3, DillHoutman1989 sentence.
   **Issue:** The outline and map assign this visual-cue study to the introduction only, and the introduction already presents it with the same caveat.
   **Recommendation:** Drop the clause here or replace it with a back-reference to the introduction.
3. **Location:** Paragraph 3, opening sentence ("depending on the receiver's situation").
   **Issue:** EilamEtAl1999 contrasts species, not situations; it fits the paragraph only loosely. The closing claim that "none of these acoustic studies measured the costs" sits awkwardly with HernandezEtAl2021, which measured feeding.
   **Recommendation:** Broaden the topic sentence to "receiver and situation", or move Eilam to paragraph 5; qualify the cost sentence to say which costs (e.g., escape cost relative to staying) were not measured.
4. **Location:** Paragraph 2, marmot sentence.
   **Issue:** Presenting added nonlinearity (BlumsteinRecapet2009) as support for threat-sensitive scaling conflates acoustic salience or unpredictability with indicated risk, a distinction the outline asks to keep.
   **Recommendation:** Add a clause noting that nonlinear components may raise responsiveness through salience rather than encoded risk.
5. **Location:** Paragraph 1, "approach indicates that a sound is attractive rather than aversive."
   **Issue:** Approach can also be investigative (inspection of a threat source), so approach does not by itself establish positive valence.
   **Recommendation:** Soften to "approach usually indicates…" or note investigative approach.
