# Review: 09_conclusion.tex

## Assessment

The conclusion does what the outline asks: it grades the evidence by strength, maps the five model components onto what is known, and turns the documented gaps into six concrete experiments that pair field and laboratory systems. Citations match the map's assignments for each hypothesis, and the applied paragraph stays within the guardrail (no validated schedule is claimed). The main weaknesses are a risk-allocation design that does not discriminate from habituation, some overstated framing of the hypotheses, and an opening summary that omits Q4.

## Changes made

- Paragraph 1: "hold across systems" changed to "are well supported," because most of the listed conclusions are specific to rats or sciurids.
- Paragraph 1: the 22-kHz sentence now reads "selective freezing ... depends on prior aversive experience" (guardrail wording) instead of "experience determines whether ... calls elicit freezing."
- Paragraph 2: "the last three are largely open" contradicted the guardrail on field habituation. It now states that habituation and identity-specific recovery are well documented in field sciurids but not against matched controls, and that timing and field outcome are open.
- Hypothesis 5: "No such test exists" changed to "We found no such test," because the map frames gaps as a search conclusion, not proof of absence.
- Hypothesis 6: "free-living sciurids" changed to "sciurids," because the Mateo2010 work is semi-natural in the map and matrix.
- Added `% VERIFY` markers for WarrenEtAl2025, LimaBednekoff1999, LillyEtAl2019, and Mateo2010. None of these has been full-text checked.

## Needs author decision

1. **Location:** Hypothesis 5 (risk allocation). **Issue:** Holding caller identity constant while increasing alarm frequency confounds risk allocation with caller-specific habituation and, if alarms are never paired with danger, with learned unreliability. Q7 says risk allocation should operate "for reasons unrelated to sensory habituation or caller reliability," but this design cannot show that. **Recommendation:** Add a discriminating element, such as rotating callers or exemplars, or testing renewal with a new caller. Alternatively, state explicitly which alternatives the design leaves open.
2. **Location:** Paragraph 2, last sentence. **Issue:** "Each designed to discriminate between competing explanations" overstates hypotheses 5 and 6. Hypothesis 6 is a research program, not a discriminating hypothesis. "Pairing field and laboratory systems" does not apply to hypothesis 4 (lab only) or hypothesis 5 (field only). **Recommendation:** Soften the framing sentence, or recast hypothesis 6 as a prediction with stated outcomes.
3. **Location:** Hypothesis 4. **Issue:** Willuhn et al. already found that 22-kHz calls did not raise accumbens dopamine. The phrase "aversive counterpart for threat calls" leaves unclear what result would count, for example a decrease like the one Furuyama et al. found for noise. **Recommendation:** State the predicted direction in each region and acknowledge the existing 22-kHz null.
4. **Location:** Paragraph 1, the Q1 sentence. **Issue:** "Carried by frequency band and gross temporal structure" generalizes the Parsana et al. neural result (frequency) and the Wöhr & Schwarting behavioral result (no AM or FM needed). "Gross temporal structure" is not a takeaway in the map. **Recommendation:** Restrict the sentence to frequency band (neural) and to the dispensability of fine modulation for approach, or cite where temporal structure was shown.
5. **Location:** Paragraph 1. **Issue:** The evidence summary skips Q4 (several endpoints, and the field costs that laboratory assays omit), which is one of the review's main contributions. **Recommendation:** Consider adding one clause that cites `\secref{sec:q4}`.
6. **Location:** Hypothesis 1. **Issue:** In Evans & Hammond, the natural-call advantage appeared at 100 dB but not at 80 dB. The prediction omits intensity. **Recommendation:** Add intensity as a factor, or specify playback at a level where the advantage was observed.
7. **Location:** Final paragraph. **Issue:** Calling the second decision "the harder one" is evaluative and unsupported. The closing sentence also leans toward a formulaic conclusion. **Recommendation:** Consider "less understood," and consider ending on the specific open question.
8. **Repetition across sections:** Paragraph 1 recaps the content of 08_synthesis (four levels, reliability directions, Davis/Herry). Hypothesis 2 restates the "decisive experiment" in 07_q7 (final paragraph). The cost-of-assessment prediction in hypothesis 3 appears in both 05_q5 and 08_synthesis. All locations were left intact; consider trimming one of each pair.
