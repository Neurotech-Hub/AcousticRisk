# Review: Q6 — Habituation and recovery (`06_q6_habituation_recovery.tex`)

## Assessment

The section answers its question rather than cataloguing studies: it separates stimulus-specific habituation (well supported in field sciurids) from a significance-dependent change in habituation rate (supported only by Evans & Hammond 1983), and it respects the map's guardrails on Berz et al. (2022) and on "thin" evidence. Citations match the map and evidence matrix. The main weakness is that the opening names four processes behind a falling response but the section only says how to separate two of them (habituation versus reliability learning; habituation versus fatigue).

## Changes made

- Paragraph 1: added `% VERIFY` above the Rankin et al. (2009) criteria sentence (index full-text check is "—").
- Paragraph 2 (Hare 1998): replaced a sentence restating the exemplar/caller result with one short inference sentence.
- Final paragraph: replaced "contradicted for within-session approach to an appetitive call" with wording that matches the map ("significance did not prevent habituation"). Berz et al. had no matched control, so it shows that habituation occurs, not that its rate is unchanged; the matrix also does not establish that the decline was within-session.

## Needs author decision

1. **Location:** Paragraph 1 and final paragraph. **Issue:** The outline asks the section to state which designs can tell the four processes apart. The caller-switch design (habituation versus reliability) and the Rankin criteria (versus fatigue) are covered, but reduced motivational significance and temporal expectation are not. **Recommendation:** Add one sentence each, for example that motivation can be probed by testing a different response to the same sound, and timing by changing the schedule at test (pointing to Q7).
2. **Location:** Paragraph 3 (Evans & Hammond). **Issue:** "All stimuli habituated rapidly within a session" may imply that significance had no effect on the within-session rate. If so, that is an informative null that bears directly on the question but is not stated. **Recommendation:** After checking the article (see verification), state whether within-session rates differed between the squeal and the mimic.
3. **Location:** Paragraphs 1 and 3–5 versus `08_synthesis.tex` (the paragraph beginning "A declining response across repeated playbacks…" and the next two). **Issue:** The synthesis repeats the four-process list, the Rankin criteria sentence, the Evans & Hammond recovery result, and the Berz/Willuhn counterexamples almost verbatim. The final Q6 paragraph's test design also repeats hypothesis 1 in `09_conclusion.tex`. **Recommendation:** Keep the evidence in Q6 and have the synthesis refer back to it; shorten one of the two experimental designs.
4. **Location:** Paragraph 4, Blumstein & Récapet sentence. **Issue:** Nonlinear components are an acoustic-salience manipulation, not ecological significance, so the sentence does not fit the paragraph's point that significance does not guarantee persistence. **Recommendation:** Label it explicitly as salience, or move it to the closing paragraph as an untested alternative route to persistence.
5. **Location:** Final paragraph, "the response that matters is whether a sound regains its effect after silence, not how strongly it is first expressed." **Issue:** This is the authors' functional argument stated as fact. The rate of within-bout decline could also matter for a missed threat. **Recommendation:** Present it as the authors' prediction (for example, "we expect…").
6. **Location:** Paragraph 2. **Issue:** The limitations are not stated: Hare (1998) tested about 14 receivers per series, juvenile females only, and the two field studies used very different intervals (about 3 min versus about 10 s). HareAtkins2001 (map role: contrast) is not cited here. **Recommendation:** Optionally add one clause on sample and timescale. A pointer to Q5 is enough for HareAtkins2001.

## Needs human verification

- EvansHammond1983a: whether recovery after 1- and 7-day rests and the 24-h spacing effect were tested at 80 dB as well as 100 dB. The text says the natural-call advantage "appeared only at the higher of two sound levels," but the matrix records the 100-vs-80 dB contrast for initial orienting. Also check whether within-session habituation rates differed between the squeal and the mimic.
- BerzEtAl2022: whether the habituation was within or across sessions, and whether a non-contingent playback control existed (the matrix lists controls as "nr").
- BlumsteinDaniel2004: the inter-call interval ("about once every 10 s") and the endpoint (foraging suppression).
- Open `% VERIFY` markers: RankinEtAl2009 (criteria); EvansHammond1983b (context generalization depends on significance); BlumsteinRecapet2009 (anti-habituation function not tested with repeated exposure).
