# Review: Q7 — Temporal expectation (`07_q7_temporal_expectation.tex`)

## Assessment

The section answers its question and keeps the empirical conclusion as narrow as the outline requires. Risk allocation and hazard are presented as theory; Davis (1970) and Herry et al. (2007) are described accurately against the evidence matrix and guardrails; the field paragraph notes the age-dependent null. The main weaknesses are a thin bridge from the dopamine evidence to *timing* specifically, no diagnostic that separates prediction from the other Part III processes, and repetition with the Introduction, Q3, Synthesis, and Conclusion.

## Needs author decision

1. **Location:** Paragraph 4 (dopamine). **Issue:** Goedhoop et al. manipulated whether a cue predicted the noise, not when the noise would arrive. Kutlu et al. concern novelty. So "depends on what the animal expected" supports prediction in general, not temporal expectation. **Recommendation:** Say plainly that neither study varied timing statistics, or cut the paragraph to one sentence and point to \secref{sec:q3}, which already describes Goedhoop in detail.
2. **Location:** Paragraphs 1–2. **Issue:** The outline asks each Part III section to say how its mechanism would be told apart experimentally from habituation (Q6) and reliability (Q5). Q7 asserts the difference ("for reasons unrelated to sensory habituation") but gives no diagnostic. Risk allocation is also a functional hypothesis that habituation could implement, so "unrelated" mixes the functional and proximate levels. **Recommendation:** Add one sentence with a discriminating prediction. For example, a prediction account implies that responding recovers when an acoustically identical sound arrives at an unexpected time; habituation and reliability accounts do not. Also soften "unrelated to" to "not reducible to".
3. **Location:** Paragraph 2, ordering prediction. **Issue:** The ranking (regular < irregular < constant hazard) treats "irregular" as one category. Uniform jitter still has a rising hazard. Decreasing-hazard (heavy-tailed) schedules, which the final paragraph lists, are not placed in the ranking. **Recommendation:** Say "uniformly jittered" rather than "irregular", and either place heavy-tailed schedules in the ranking or state that the concept does not rank them.
4. **Location:** Paragraph 3, last sentence. **Issue:** The text says nothing tests whether meaningful sounds interact differently with timing. EvansHammond1983a (mapped to Q7 as "significance interacts with long spacing") shows a significance × spacing interaction for interval length, though not for predictability. **Recommendation:** Add one clause citing \citet{EvansHammond1983a} with a pointer to \secref{sec:q6}, framed as interval length, not regularity.
5. **Location:** Paragraph 5 (SloanHare2006). **Issue:** The adult null could reflect adults discounting juvenile callers rather than failing to decode rate. If so, it is a reliability or caller-class effect (Q5), not a temporal-decoding one. **Recommendation:** Name this alternative in one clause.
6. **Repetition across sections.** The risk allocation description and its "not tested with acoustic cues in rodents" caveat appear in 00_introduction (paragraph with \citep{LimaBednekoff1999}), 08_synthesis, and here. The Goedhoop result appears in 03_q3 (two paragraphs) and here. The matched-schedule experiment in the final paragraph duplicates hypothesis 2 ("Hazard rather than variability") in 09_conclusion. The Davis/Herry summary is repeated in 08_synthesis. **Recommendation:** Keep the full treatment in Q7 and shorten the others to cross-references, or shorten the experiment sentence here and point to the Conclusion.

If they approximate Poisson timing, the section's claim that distribution shape was untested needs qualifying (the guardrail itself may then need author review).
