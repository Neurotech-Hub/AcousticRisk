# Review: 02_q2_innate_vs_learned.tex

## Assessment

The section answers its question rather than cataloguing studies. It treats the Hendrie–Kindermann conflict and 22-kHz experience dependence the way the guardrails require, and it gives the null and conflicting results their proper weight. The weak points are a few interpretive steps that go further than the data, especially on 22-kHz "intrinsic salience" and the developmental synthesis, and a fair amount of material repeated from Q1, Q3, Q4, and Q5.

## Changes made

- Predator calls, paragraph 1: Kindermann rat conditioning now says "after ten pairings", following the map and matrix. One-trial learning occurred for only two predators and one non-predator.
- 22-kHz paragraph: added "largely by frequency" to the Parsana et al. (2012a) amygdala claim (guardrail).
- Eavesdropping, last sentence: replaced "carry more information about current risk" with the map's wording (an indirect cue can outweigh a direct predator cue; chipmunks changed foraging more).
- Function paragraph: the innate-recognition prediction is now marked as the authors' extension of error management rather than attributed to Nesse (2005). The map notes that Nesse does not predict which cues are privileged.
- Function paragraph, conclusion: "acquires its defensive meaning" became "acquires the capacity to evoke selective freezing" (guardrail; Olszyński et al. 2020 show non-freezing effects without conditioning).
- Added `% VERIFY` markers for MagrathEtAl2015, SchmidtEtAl2008, and Blumstein2006, none of which has been full-text checked.

## Needs author decision

1. **Location:** 22-kHz paragraph 1, the sentence on Endres et al. (2007). **Issue:** Greater persistence of learned fear is evidence of preparedness for association. It is not evidence of acoustic *salience*, so the inference conflates two concepts the outline asks us to keep separate. The sentence also sits after Kim et al. rather than with Endres. **Recommendation:** Say "a predisposition to retain learned fear to the 22-kHz band" and move the sentence next to the Endres result. Carry the same wording into the closing synthesis ("some intrinsic salience").
2. **Location:** 22-kHz paragraph 1, Kim et al. (2010). **Issue:** The text says the partner's calls "were played to receivers" but then says the partner's other fear behaviours confound the result. Those two statements cannot both describe a single playback design. **Recommendation:** State which part of the study used live pairs and which, if any, used playback (see verification list), then attach the confound only to the live-pair part.
3. **Location:** Development, last sentence. **Issue:** "Both fit a sequence in which an initial bias is refined by experience" goes beyond Mateo (1996), which shows physiology leading behaviour but no demonstrated bias plus calibration. Schiavo et al. show adult maternal calibration, which is not developmental. **Recommendation:** Narrow the claim to the dissociation between discrimination and overt response, or state what Mateo shows about the role of experience once it has been verified.
4. **Location:** Olszyński et al. (2020) sentence. **Issue:** Computer-generated tones had effects in the same direction, so the non-freezing effects in naïve rats may be frequency-band effects rather than call recognition. **Recommendation:** Add a clause saying so; this also supports item 1.
5. **Location:** Section close. **Issue:** The introduction adopts a three-stage working model, but the conclusion never evaluates it explicitly. **Recommendation:** Add one sentence stating which stages each case supports.
6. **Location:** Introduction, `MagrathEtAl2015`. **Issue:** This is an eavesdropping review, but it is cited for a general claim about all sound classes. **Recommendation:** Limit the citation to heterospecific cues, or move it to the eavesdropping subsection.
7. **Cross-section repetition (both locations left intact):**
   - Lilly et al. safety cue: Q2 eavesdropping and Q5, near-verbatim. It is also tangential to the innate-versus-learned question. Consider keeping it only in Q5.
   - Mateo (1996): Q2 development and Q3 paragraph 2.
   - Mateo (2010): Q2 development and Q4.
   - Eilam et al. spiny mice: Q2 field paragraph, Q3, Q4, and the synthesis text and figure caption.
   - Parsana et al. (2012a): Q1, Q2, Q3, and Q4.
   - Schmidt et al. chipmunks: Q1 and Q2. Already cross-referenced; consider citing only via `\secref`.
   - Hendrie–Kindermann summary: Q2 and synthesis paragraph 3.
