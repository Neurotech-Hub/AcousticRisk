# Review: 08_synthesis.tex

## Assessment

The section does what the outline asks: it organizes the review around four dissociable levels and turns the updating literature into contrasting predictions. That makes it the manuscript's main conceptual contribution. Most citations match the map and evidence matrix, and the guardrails on reliability direction, familiarity, Herry's jitter, Davis, risk allocation, and dopamine intensity scaling are respected. The main weaknesses are that much of the level-by-level material repeats Q3 and Q4, that the critical-distinctions table is still missing, and that one of the four updating processes never gets a diagnostic prediction.

## Changes made

- **Four levels, opening paragraph:** changed "each pair of adjacent levels has been experimentally dissociated" to "several of these levels". The section does not show a dissociation between action selection and updating, and the map only says the levels "can dissociate".
- **Interpretation paragraph, predator calls:** changed "strain as well as paradigm" to "species, strain, and paradigm", following the guardrail wording.
- **Interpretation paragraph, caller identity:** the sentence introduced as field evidence cited lab rats and prairie voles. It now separates sciurids in the field (Hare1998, BlumsteinDaniel2004) from rats and voles in the laboratory (VielleEtAl2021, WarrenEtAl2025).
- **Action-selection paragraph:** changed "match the economics of escape" to "are consistent with". These studies did not measure the costs the model requires, as Q4 itself states.
- **Significance paragraph:** cut "exactly". Added the map's qualifier to WilluhnEtAl2014: that design cannot separate habituation from falling motivation.
- **VERIFY markers:** added 8 lines, covering 9 papers whose full text has not been checked (listed below).

## Needs author decision

1. **Location:** final paragraph of the updating subsection. **Issue:** four processes are introduced (habituation, unreliability, reduced motivational significance, temporal expectation), but only three get diagnostic predictions, and only three quantities are said to be "updated". Reduced motivation is left without a test, even though Q6 and the Willuhn qualifier both rely on it. **Recommendation:** say what would distinguish a motivational decline (for example, recovery after a change in deprivation state, with no change in stimulus or source), or state explicitly that current designs cannot separate it.
2. **Location:** end of the section (`% TODO`). **Issue:** the critical-distinctions table in OUTLINE.md is not drafted, and Figure 1 does not cover it. **Recommendation:** draft it, or decide to drop it. If you draft it, keep the rows the text does not already make (conspecific alarm versus predator vocalization, startle versus avoidance, unpredictable timing versus longer recovery).
3. **Location:** "Neural measures" paragraph, and the action-selection paragraph. **Issue:** the Furuyama/Goedhoop comparison and the Freeman c-Fos result restate Q3 (03_q3_neural_encoding.tex, dopamine paragraph). The Ydenberg–Dill sentence is nearly verbatim from Q4 (04_q4_behavioral_expression.tex, line 17). Mongeau and Eilam are also repeated from Q4 and Q2. **Recommendation:** in the synthesis, cite each result in one clause as an instance of a level dissociation, and keep the detailed comparison in Q3 and Q4.
4. **Location:** interpretation paragraph, predator-call conflict. **Issue:** the map calls HendrieNeill1991 "a fragile" positive result, since the same group later found no fear in untreated DBA/2 mice. The synthesis presents the conflict as evenly balanced. **Recommendation:** consider a clause noting this fragility, if Q2 does not already carry it.
5. **Location:** opening paragraph of the four-levels subsection. **Issue:** the outline asks the synthesis to map report E's hierarchical inference model and draft equation onto the levels, "as a proposal only". Neither appears. **Recommendation:** decide whether to add them (a box or the supplement) or drop them from the outline.
6. **Location:** interpretation paragraph. **Issue:** "Field studies add dimensions that laboratory designs rarely manipulate" now introduces a sentence that also cites laboratory identity studies. Single-newline joins also fold the field material, the Freeman sentence, and the marmot reliability sentences into the preceding paragraphs. **Recommendation:** decide whether these are meant as separate paragraphs, and reword the field lead-in if needed.
