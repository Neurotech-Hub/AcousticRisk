# Part A — Standard Manuscript Figure Aesthetic

**Apply consistently to every figure in the manuscript.**

### Illustration style
- Naturalistic biological illustration combining fine pencil-like linework, delicate watercolor shading, and anatomically accurate forms.
- Match the supplied chipmunk, gray squirrel, ground squirrel, rat, spiny mouse, and vole portraits.
- Preserve species-specific anatomy, proportions, coloration, and fur texture.
- Use the supplied landscape illustration as a reference for the overall artistic sensibility, not as a mandatory background.
- Animals should look like illustrations from a contemporary scientific field guide, not photographs, cartoons, or generic vector clip art.

### Graphic design
- Pure white background for scientific diagrams.
- Flat, uniform fills for diagram panels and graphical elements.
- Restrained natural-history palette: warm ivory, ochre, sage, muted teal, dusty lavender, and charcoal.
- Fine, consistent arrow and connector strokes.
- Generous whitespace and minimal visual clutter.
- Prefer open compositions rather than rounded infographic cards.
- No gradients, decorative shadows, artificial lighting, glossy effects, or unnecessary icons.

### Typography
- Consistent sans-serif typography.
- Minimal text, generally restricted to short labels.
- Consistent panel lettering (A, B, C).
- No embedded figure titles, figure numbers, citations, or captions.
- The manuscript caption provides the detailed explanation.

### Composition
- Landscape orientation unless specified otherwise.
- Illustrations should communicate the scientific concepts; arrows, labels, and diagrams provide structure.
- Maintain consistent animal illustration scale, rendering detail, and color treatment.
- Reuse supplied animal assets when practical; generate additional poses in the same visual style when necessary.
- Use graphical conventions consistently across all figures.

### Quality standard
The result should resemble an illustrated scientific review or natural-history publication, with professional diagrammatic organization. Avoid recognizable AI-infographic conventions.

**Primary aesthetic reference:** Supplied natural-history landscape and species portraits. These take precedence over generic BioRender styling.
