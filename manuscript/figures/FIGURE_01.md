# Figure 1 — Receiver-Centered Model of Acoustic Significance

## Style Reference

Follow the aesthetic specifications in `FIGURE_STYLE.md` exactly. Use the supplied natural-history animal portraits as the primary references for illustration style, anatomical detail, coloration, and texture.

Do not introduce a competing visual style. All figures in this manuscript must share a consistent appearance.

## Scientific Objective

Illustrate how rodents detect acoustic information, interpret its biological significance, select behavioral and physiological responses, and experience ecological consequences.

The central message is that **acoustic detection, interpretation, and response are distinguishable but interacting processes**, rather than stages of a strictly serial pathway.

The figure must communicate three important relationships:

1. Acoustic interpretation depends on experience, source reliability, and ecological familiarity.
2. Rapid defensive responses can occur without learned interpretation.
3. Experience, repetition, and ecological consequences influence subsequent responses.

This is a conceptual model, not a demonstrated neural circuit.

## Layout

Create a clean, landscape-oriented schematic containing four components arranged horizontally.

**Detection → Interpretation → Action selection → Ecological consequence**

Use a white background with generous whitespace. Prefer an open composition with subtle visual grouping rather than large colored rectangular panels.

### 1. Detection

Illustrate a naturalistic brown rodent receiving an incoming acoustic waveform.

- Show one restrained, scientifically recognizable waveform.
- Emphasize the physical detection of sound rather than its assigned meaning.
- Label: **Detection**

Use muted teal as the associated accent color.

### 2. Interpretation

Illustrate the same rodent in a neutral, attentive posture.

Represent three interacting determinants:

- Experience
- Source reliability
- Ecological familiarity

These may appear as three short labels arranged around the animal, without individual boxes or decorative icons.

Do not depict interpretation as conscious reasoning. Avoid thought bubbles, question marks, or anthropomorphic expressions.

Label: **Interpretation**

Use warm ochre as the accent color.

### 3. Action Selection

Illustrate four distinct behavioral outcomes using small, anatomically accurate rodents:

- **Vigilance:** Upright, alert posture.
- **Freezing:** Stationary, low posture without locomotion.
- **Flight:** Running or leaping away.
- **Approach:** Deliberate movement toward a stimulus.

Arrange the four illustrations in a compact, balanced grouping. Maintain consistent species appearance, illustration scale, and rendering quality.

Label: **Action selection**

Use muted lavender as the accent color.

### 4. Ecological Consequence

Illustrate three possible ecological outcomes:

- **Foraging:** A rodent feeding or investigating food.
- **Refuge:** A rodent entering or occupying a burrow.
- **Social interaction:** Two rodents interacting.

Use minimal landscape details, such as sparse grass, a natural burrow, or a few seeds. These should establish ecological context without becoming decorative scenery.

Label: **Ecological consequence**

Use sage green as the accent color.

## Conceptual Connections

### Primary pathway

Connect the four components using thin, consistent charcoal arrows.

**Detection → Interpretation → Action selection → Ecological consequence**

Arrows indicate conceptual relationships, not obligatory neural connections.

### Rapid response pathway

Add a secondary pathway connecting **Detection directly to Action selection**, visually bypassing Interpretation.

Label: **Rapid response**

Place this pathway above the primary sequence. Use a thin, unobtrusive arrow.

It represents rapid startle and defensive reactions to salient acoustic events that need not depend on learned meaning.

### Experience-dependent updating

Add one feedback pathway from **Ecological consequence** back to **Interpretation**.

Label: **Updating**

Place this pathway beneath the primary sequence.

It represents how experience, repetition, source reliability, and outcomes can modify future interpretation.

### Physiological response

Include a small, distinct branch from acoustic processing representing physiological changes that may occur without overt behavioral responses.

Use a single restrained cardiac waveform or equivalent physiological illustration.

Label: **Physiology**

Position this element below the main sequence without disrupting the feedback pathway. Do not imply that physiological responses are necessarily downstream of behavioral action.

## Visual Priorities

- Naturalistic rodent artwork should be the dominant visual element.
- Use species portraits and animal poses consistent with the supplied reference imagery.
- Keep all illustrations scientifically credible and stylistically unified.
- Minimize text and eliminate decorative symbols.
- Use solid, uniform graphical fills; preserve delicate watercolor and pencil texture only within biological illustrations.
- Maintain consistent line weights, spacing, and typography.
- Make the rapid response pathway visually distinct from the primary pathway.
- Make feedback immediately understandable without allowing it to dominate the composition.

## Explicit Exclusions

Do not include:

- An embedded figure title or figure number.
- A caption or explanatory paragraph.
- A legend.
- Large colored cards or rounded infographic panels.
- Gradients, drop shadows, glows, or colored background textures.
- Thought bubbles, question marks, decorative brains, or generic icons.
- Unnecessary subheadings or explanatory text.
- Photorealistic, cartoon, or generic AI-infographic aesthetics.

## Final Output

Produce a high-resolution, landscape scientific illustration suitable for a neuroscience review manuscript.

The figure should be understandable primarily through the arrangement of animals, acoustic information, arrows, and ecological scenes. Text should clarify relationships rather than explain them.

**Success criterion:** A reader should immediately understand that acoustic information can produce different responses depending on experience and context, that rapid responses can bypass learned interpretation, and that subsequent experience updates future responses.

Preserve a clean, cohesive natural-history illustration aesthetic suitable for reuse across the manuscript's complete figure series.
